Suno AI Pricing 2026: Every Plan Explained

Suno AI pricing in 2026: every plan explained with honest credit math. Free vs Pro vs Premier - which tier fits your creator workflow without overpaying.

Suno AI pricing is simple on the surface - three tiers, one credit system - but the details decide whether you're overpaying. I've run Suno's free, Pro, and Premier plans across real creator workloads: YouTube background music, podcast intros, short-form beds. This guide breaks down every plan, does the credit math honestly, and tells you which tier fits which creator in 2026.

Pricing is at time of writing. Suno adjusts plans and credit costs, so treat the numbers below as current-as-of-October-2026 and verify on suno.com/pricing before you subscribe.

Basic (Free) - $0

The free tier refreshes 50 credits every day, which works out to roughly 10 songs daily. That's genuinely generous for learning: you can test genres, practice prompting, and figure out whether Suno fits your workflow without spending anything. The limits that matter: no commercial use rights (Suno retains ownership of free-tier output), access to an older model rather than the current v5.5, and a shared generation queue that's slower at peak times.

Pro - $10/month ($8/month annual)

Pro is the plan most creators should buy. You get 2,500 credits a month - around 500 songs - access to the current v5.5 model, stem separation for pulling vocals and instruments apart, and full commercial rights to everything you generate while subscribed. Annual billing drops the effective price to $8/month, a 20 percent saving if you're committed.

Premier - $30/month ($24/month annual)

Premier quadruples the credit pool to 10,000 a month (roughly 2,000 songs) and unlocks Suno Studio, the built-in editing workspace with deeper stem and MIDI-style control. This tier only makes sense if you're generating at serious volume or you specifically need Studio's editing tools. For everyone else, it's overkill.

The Credit Math, Honestly

Suno meters everything in credits, and the number that matters is simple: one song generation costs about 5 credits and returns two takes. That makes every plan easy to translate. Free's 50 daily credits mean about 10 songs a day. Pro's 2,500 monthly credits mean about 500 songs a month. Premier's 10,000 mean about 2,000.

The catch is that editing costs extra. Extending a song, regenerating sections, and Studio actions all draw from the same pool, so a heavy editing session burns credits faster than the per-song math suggests. In my experience, budget roughly 30 percent of your credits for iteration, not just first drafts.

Two more rules worth knowing: credits don't roll over between billing cycles, so unused credits expire. And add-on credit packs are available for purchase on paid plans if you blow through your monthly pool - useful for deadline months, but if you're buying top-ups regularly, that's a sign you should be on the next tier up.

Which Plan for Which Creator

  • Just exploring AI music: Free. Fifty daily credits is plenty to learn prompting and find your sound. Don't pay until you have a project.
  • YouTuber / podcaster needing background music: Pro. Commercial rights are the whole point, and 500 songs a month is more than a weekly publishing schedule needs.
  • Short-form creator (TikTok, Reels, Shorts): Pro. You need licensable beds constantly, and the per-song cost at Pro is a few cents.
  • Musician prototyping songs: Pro to start; Premier if you're using Suno Studio's stem editing as part of a real production workflow.
  • Agency or high-volume production: Premier. The 10,000-credit pool and Studio access are built for this.

The Free-to-Pro Jump Is the One That Matters

The meaningful upgrade on Suno isn't Pro to Premier - it's free to Pro. That single step buys you three things that change what the tool is: commercial rights, the current model, and stem separation. Everything before that step is practice; everything after it is production.

The most expensive mistake I see: creators who build a catalog of tracks on the free tier, then upgrade and assume their old songs are now licensed. They're not. Free-tier generations stay non-commercial forever. If you're on free and thinking about going Pro, start generating your keepers after the upgrade. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does Suno AI cost per month?

Suno has three tiers: Free ($0), Pro ($10/month or $8/month billed annually), and Premier ($30/month or $24/month billed annually). Annual billing saves about 20 percent on both paid plans. Prices are at time of writing - verify on Suno's pricing page.

How many songs do you get with Suno Pro?

Pro includes 2,500 credits per month, and a song generation costs about 5 credits, so roughly 500 songs. Editing, extending, and regenerating sections also cost credits, so real-world output is lower if you iterate heavily.

Do Suno credits roll over?

No. Monthly subscription credits expire at the end of each billing cycle. Separately purchased add-on credits don't expire but require an active subscription to spend.

Is Suno Pro worth it for YouTubers?

Yes, if you publish regularly. Pro's commercial license covers monetized videos, and 500 songs a month far exceeds what a weekly channel needs. The per-song cost works out to a few cents - far cheaper than licensing stock music per track.

Can I cancel Suno anytime?

Yes, subscriptions are month-to-month unless you choose annual billing. Note that songs keep the license from when they were generated - tracks made during a paid month retain their commercial rights after you cancel, though you should keep your invoices as proof.

Final Verdict

For most creators, the decision is binary: free while you're learning, Pro the moment money enters the picture. Pro at $8 to $10 a month is one of the better values in creative AI - 500 licensable songs a month for less than the cost of two stock music licenses.

Premier is a specialist tier. Unless you're generating hundreds of tracks monthly or living inside Suno Studio's editing tools, save the $20 difference. And whatever you do, don't build a commercial catalog on the free tier expecting to license it later. Start clean on Pro, keep your receipts, and check current pricing before every renewal - this market moves fast.
