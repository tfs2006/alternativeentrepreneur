9 Best Recurring Affiliate Programs 2026: Earn Monthly

Recurring affiliate programs pay you every month your referral stays subscribed. Here are 9 real SaaS programs with lifetime recurring commissions.

The best recurring affiliate programs are the closest thing this industry has to compounding returns. Instead of earning once per sale, you earn a percentage of every payment your referral makes - month after month, for as long as they stay subscribed. Refer 50 people to a $30/month tool at 30% commission and you're looking at roughly $450/month in recurring income from work you did once. That math is why experienced affiliates quietly prefer SaaS programs over one-time payouts.

This list covers real, currently operating software programs with recurring commission structures at time of writing. I've focused on tools with genuine staying power - because a recurring commission is only as good as the product's retention rate. A program paying 50% on software everyone cancels in month two is worse than one paying 20% on software people keep for years.

Why Recurring Commissions Win

One-time commissions reset your income to zero every month. Recurring commissions stack. In year one the difference looks small; by year three, an affiliate with 200 active software referrals can earn a full-time income from maintenance content alone while a one-time-commission affiliate is still grinding for every sale. The tradeoff: recurring programs are usually SaaS tools with free trials and longer sales cycles, so conversions take more trust-building. Tutorial and comparison content is the format that works - which pairs well with AI tools for affiliate marketing to speed up production.

The 9 Best Recurring Affiliate Programs

1. Kit (formerly ConvertKit)

Kit pays 30% recurring commission on its email marketing plans. Email tools have excellent retention - once a creator's list lives in Kit, they rarely migrate - which makes this one of the most stable recurring programs available. Perfect if your audience includes bloggers, YouTubers, or newsletter writers.

2. GetResponse

GetResponse offers a choice: 33% recurring commissions or a $100 flat bounty per sale. Take the recurring option unless you need cash now. It's a full email marketing and automation platform, so the audience is broad: small businesses, course creators, and ecommerce stores.

3. ActiveCampaign

ActiveCampaign pays 20-30% recurring depending on your tier, on a premium marketing automation platform. The higher price point means bigger absolute commissions per referral. Best suited to affiliates whose audience is beyond the beginner stage - agencies, established businesses, serious email marketers.

4. AWeber

AWeber pays up to 30% recurring commissions and has been around since 1998, which matters for retention. It's the "boring reliable" email tool, and boring reliable keeps subscribers - and your commissions - for years. Good fit for small business and solopreneur audiences.

5. HubSpot

HubSpot's program pays 30% recurring for up to one year per referral. The one-year cap is the catch, but HubSpot's deal sizes are large - 30% of a Professional-tier subscription for twelve months adds up fast. Ideal for B2B and marketing-agency audiences.

6. Teachable

Teachable pays 30% recurring on course-platform subscriptions. The online course market keeps growing, and creators who launch on Teachable tend to stay put once their school is built. If your audience wants to sell knowledge products, this is a natural recommendation.

7. ClickFunnels

ClickFunnels pays 30% recurring on funnel-building software subscriptions. The affiliate community around it is enormous, which means competition - but also endless proof that the product retains. Works best if you genuinely use funnels in your own business and can show real workflows.

8. Systeme.io

Systeme.io pays 60% recurring - one of the highest rates in SaaS affiliate marketing. It's an all-in-one marketing platform popular with beginners because of its free tier. High commission plus a free entry point makes it convert well, though average subscription values are lower than premium tools.

9. Jasper

Jasper pays 30% recurring on its AI writing platform subscriptions. AI tools have strong word-of-mouth dynamics, and Jasper's brand recognition in the creator space helps conversions. A natural fit if you already create content about AI tools and workflows.

How to Promote Recurring Programs Honestly

Recurring programs punish hype and reward honesty. If you oversell a SaaS tool, the customer cancels in month two and your "recurring" income evaporates. What works: publish genuine tutorials showing your actual workflow, compare the tool against alternatives fairly (including when a competitor wins), and disclose the affiliate relationship clearly. Content that helps someone succeed with the tool directly protects your commission stream - a customer who gets value doesn't cancel. For niche selection around software, affiliate marketing niche research covers how to validate demand before you commit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does "lifetime recurring commission" actually mean?

It means you earn a percentage of each subscription payment for as long as the customer keeps paying - not just the first month. If they stay three years, you get paid for three years. Some programs cap this (HubSpot pays for 12 months); most on this list pay indefinitely.

Are recurring affiliate programs better than high one-time payouts?

Over time, usually yes. A $500 one-time payout beats a $15/month recurring commission for the first 33 months - then the recurring wins forever. Recurring income also smooths out the feast-or-famine cycle that burns out affiliates.

Can beginners join recurring affiliate programs?

Yes. Most SaaS affiliate programs accept new affiliates; they care more about content quality than audience size. Systeme.io, Kit, and AWeber are particularly approachable. Start with tools you actually use so your content is genuine.

What happens to my commissions if a customer cancels?

They stop - you only earn while the subscription is active. This is why promoting quality tools with good retention matters more than chasing the highest commission percentage. A 20% commission on software people keep beats 50% on software they abandon.

Do I need a website to promote SaaS affiliate programs?

A website helps enormously because tutorial and comparison content ranks in search for years, quietly generating trials. YouTube works too - software tutorials are one of the highest-converting video formats. Either way, searchable evergreen content beats social posts that vanish in a day.

Final Verdict

If I had to rebuild from zero with one strategy, it would be this: pick two or three SaaS tools you genuinely use, join their recurring programs, and publish in-depth tutorials and honest comparisons. Kit or Systeme.io for the creator/beginner crowd, ActiveCampaign or HubSpot if your audience is more advanced.

The unsexy truth about recurring affiliate income: it's slow for six months and then it compounds. Affiliates who quit in month four never see the curve bend. Commit to a year of consistent tutorial content around two solid tools - check high-ticket affiliate marketing too if you want bigger per-sale numbers alongside the recurring base - and you'll understand why the veterans all end up here.
