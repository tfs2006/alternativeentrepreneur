Best Affiliate Networks 2026: Impact vs ShareASale vs CJ

The best affiliate networks are marketplaces that connect you with hundreds or thousands of brands through a single account, one dashboard, and consolidated payouts. Instead of applying to fifty individual programs, you join a network once and then apply to merchants inside it. For beginners this is a massive shortcut - and for experienced affiliates, networks are where the serious brand deals live.

I've worked with all four major networks for years. This comparison covers Impact, ShareASale, CJ Affiliate, and Awin: what each does best, what it costs you (spoiler: joining is free on all of them), payout thresholds, and which one deserves your first application. If you're brand new, start with affiliate marketing for beginners first, then come back here to choose your network.

What Affiliate Networks Actually Do

A network sits between you and the merchant. It provides the tracking links, records your clicks and sales, handles the money, and pays you - usually monthly once you clear a threshold. The merchant pays the network; the network pays you. This matters because it means one trusted payer instead of dozens of small companies you have to chase. Networks also give you a searchable directory of programs with commission rates, cookie lengths, and performance data (like average earnings per click) so you can evaluate merchants before applying.

Impact

Impact has become the network of choice for major direct-to-consumer brands - think Shopify-adjacent SaaS companies, big retailers, and recognizable names. Its interface is the most modern of the four, with clean reporting and genuinely useful data on which merchants convert. Payout threshold is low and payments are reliable. The catch: premium brands can be pickier about approvals, so brand-new sites sometimes get declined by the biggest names. Apply to mid-size merchants first, build a track record, then go after the flagships.

ShareASale

ShareASale (owned by Awin) is the beginner's best friend. Thousands of merchants, many of them small to mid-size businesses that approve new affiliates quickly. The interface looks dated, but the merchant search and filtering actually work well, and the $50 payout threshold is reachable. Where ShareASale really shines is niche coverage - if you run an odd, specific site (hobby gear, specialty foods, obscure B2B tools), you'll find merchants here that exist nowhere else.

CJ Affiliate

CJ (formerly Commission Junction) is the old guard - one of the largest networks, home to major retail and travel brands. Its advertiser roster skews toward big companies, which means strong brand recognition but stricter approvals and, frankly, a clunky interface that hasn't kept pace. Still worth joining for the big-name retail programs alone; just expect the application process to feel more corporate than the others.

Awin

Awin is the global heavyweight, especially strong in the UK and Europe, and it owns ShareASale in the US. If your audience is international, Awin's merchant base is unmatched - including programs like Etsy that run through it. Note the small refundable signup deposit (around $5 at time of writing, returned with your first payout), which exists to filter out spam accounts. Don't let it put you off; it's standard.

Which Network Should You Join First?

Join ShareASale first. It's free, approvals are friendly to new affiliates, and you'll find merchants in nearly any niche within an hour of searching. Add Impact second once you have some content live - its brand roster is where the higher-value partnerships live. Add CJ and Awin as you identify specific merchants you want; there's no benefit to joining a network with no programs you plan to promote. This isn't a loyalty decision - most working affiliates hold accounts on all four and promote wherever the best merchant for their content lives. The network is plumbing; the merchant relationship is what earns.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do affiliate networks cost money to join?

No - joining as an affiliate (publisher) is free on Impact, ShareASale, CJ, and Awin. Awin charges a small refundable signup deposit to deter spam accounts, returned with your first payout. Merchants pay the network fees, not you.

Can beginners get approved on these networks?

Yes, with a real website. Networks approve the account easily; individual merchants then approve your applications. Big brands may decline new sites, but hundreds of smaller merchants approve quickly. Start there and trade up as your traffic grows.

Which network pays the most?

Networks don't set commission rates - merchants do. The same brand might pay 5% on one network and 8% on another, so it pays to compare when a merchant lists on multiple networks. Your earnings depend on the merchants you choose, not the network logo.

How do network payouts work?

Earnings accrue in your network balance; once you clear the threshold ($10-$50 depending on network), you're paid on the network's schedule - usually monthly via direct deposit or PayPal. One payout can combine commissions from dozens of merchants, which beats chasing small checks individually.

Should I join multiple networks?

Eventually, yes. There's no exclusivity - join every network that hosts merchants you want to promote. But start with one (ShareASale) so you're promoting instead of drowning in dashboards. Expand when you have a specific merchant in mind, not before.

Final Verdict

For most affiliates in 2026: ShareASale for breadth and beginner-friendly approvals, Impact for premium brands and the best reporting, CJ for big-name retail programs, Awin for international reach. None of them is "the best" in isolation - the best network is the one hosting the merchants your audience buys from.

Practical next step: open a ShareASale account today, search your niche, and apply to five merchants. Then read how to find affiliate products to promote to build a repeatable process for evaluating which programs deserve your content. The network gets you in the door; your content does the selling.
